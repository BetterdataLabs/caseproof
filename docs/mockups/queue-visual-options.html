Work queue: visual options

Five ways to show the work queue counts that currently appear as plain number tiles. Every option uses the same live snapshot of the demo workspace (20 cases, 15 October 2026). Press Simulate activity to watch cases move between states and see how each option reacts.

Showing the live snapshot.

How to read this page

  • A (speedometer) is the most eye-catching. It works for one headline ratio, such as how much of the open work is waiting on the bank.
  • B (pipeline bar) and C (donut) show how all cases split across the five states.
  • D (live flow) shows each case as a dot moving between states. It's the most dynamic option.
  • E (enhanced tiles) is the smallest change to the current screen and the quickest to build.
  • Options can be combined. For example, A as a header with E below it.
Option A

Speedometers: reviewer pressure and completion

The left dial's needle shows how much of the open work is waiting on a person (needs a decision or paused). The right dial shows how much of the queue is finished. The needle swings when the numbers change.

Works well for

  • A leadership view or a wall screen
  • One question answered at a glance: are we keeping up?

Watch out for

  • A dial shows only one ratio, so the five individual counts disappear
  • The calm, busy and overloaded thresholds are placeholders that need agreeing

One bar covers every case, split by state. Each segment's length is its share of the queue, and segments grow or shrink as cases move.

Option C

Donut with headline

A ring split by state, with the total and the completion rate in the centre. Hovering a state highlights its slice.

Works well for

  • Familiar and appealing; the total is front and centre
  • The legend shows the exact counts

Watch out for

  • Slices of similar size (such as 4 and 5) are hard to compare by eye
  • Takes more space than option B for the same information
Option D

Live case flow

Every case is a dot. When the agent finishes, the business replies or a reviewer decides, its dot moves to the new state. Hover or tab to a dot to see the case and its next action.

The most recently changed case pulses. Moves are animated so you can follow a case.

Works well for

  • The most dynamic option: you can see the agent at work
  • Every case can be reached directly, a good bridge to the case list

Watch out for

  • Gets crowded beyond about 60 cases (would switch to grouped counts)
  • Movement can distract on a screen people work in all day
Option E

Enhanced tiles

Today's five tiles, each with a small bar for its share of the queue and an animated count. The tile highlights briefly when its count changes, and the Agent working icon spins while the agent is busy.

Works well for

  • The smallest change, which keeps the existing layout and click-to-filter
  • Every count stays readable

Watch out for

  • The least dramatic option, so it may not be the "wow" the lead is after
  • Shares across five tiles are harder to compare than in a single bar

Part 2

The same five numbers, for a leader

These options use only what the work queue tiles already show (Needs a decision, Agent working, Waiting for the business, Paused and Complete) and present it the way a leader reads it. They react to the same Simulate activity controls at the top.

Thinking out loud

What a leader wants from five counts

A reviewer reads the tiles one at a time: which column do I work on? A leader glances once and wants the answer, not the inventory. Without adding any new data, the five counts can answer three leader questions.

The three questions

  1. How much is done? Complete out of all cases.
  2. Where is the rest, and whose problem is it? Grouping the open states by who holds the next step: Needs a decision and Paused are with the bank, Waiting for the business is with the business, Agent working is with the agent. Only the bank's share is the leader's to fix.
  3. Should I worry? A single-word verdict based on how much of the open work sits with the bank.

How that shapes the designs

  • Lead with the answer. Open with a sentence and a verdict before any chart.
  • Show it as a hierarchy. All cases → done or open → why it's open. Flow and ring charts show this better than five separate tiles.
  • Count things people can picture. "8 of 20 documents" lands faster than "40%".
  • No new data. Every number here can be worked out from the five tiles, so nothing extra needs building in the backend.
Option F

Headline and health verdict

A verdict badge, one sentence that explains it, and three numbers. Readable in five seconds and easy to repeat in a meeting.

Verdict rule (placeholder, to agree): the share of open cases waiting on the bank. Under 33% is Healthy, 33–66% is Busy, over 66% Needs attention.

Works well for

  • The fastest possible read
  • Works as a header above any of the other options

Watch out for

  • The verdict depends on thresholds that need agreeing
  • Mostly words; pair it with G, H or I for a visual
Option G

Flow: from all cases to where each one sits

A flow diagram (Sankey). All cases split into open and complete, and open work splits into the four states, with the bank's share bracketed. The bands resize smoothly as cases move. Hover a band to see its count.

Works well for

  • Shows the whole story in one picture: done, not done, and why
  • Looks impressive and is still easy to read

Watch out for

  • Needs width; on mobile it would stack into option B
  • States with zero cases show as a thin line
Option H

Two rings: done vs not done, and why

The inner ring answers "how much is done?" and the outer ring breaks the open part into states. Hover a row or a slice to highlight it.

Works well for

  • Compact, and the headline percentage is in the centre
  • The ring is familiar, with the extra "why" layer that a plain donut (C) lacks

Watch out for

  • Slices of similar size are hard to compare by eye; the legend shows the counts
  • Two rings is the limit; a third would get hard to read
Option I

One document per case

A pictogram: every case is a document icon, grouped by state. People count icons without reading an axis, and new or moved cases pop in so changes are easy to spot.

Works well for

  • The most human and friendly option, good for presentations
  • Each icon can link to its case

Watch out for

  • Works up to about 60 cases; beyond that one icon would stand for 5 or 10 cases
  • Less precise for comparing proportions than B or G
