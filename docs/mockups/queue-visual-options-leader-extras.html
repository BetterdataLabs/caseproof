Work queue: visual options

Five ways to show the work queue counts that currently appear as plain number tiles. Every option uses the same live snapshot of the demo workspace (20 cases, 15 October 2026). Press Simulate activity to watch cases move between states and see how each option reacts.

Showing the live snapshot.

How to read this page

  • A (speedometer) is the most eye-catching. It works for one headline ratio, such as how much of the open work is waiting on the bank.
  • B (pipeline bar) and C (donut) show how all cases split across the five states.
  • D (live flow) shows each case as a dot moving between states. It's the most dynamic option.
  • E (enhanced tiles) is the smallest change to the current screen and the quickest to build.
  • Options can be combined. For example, A as a header with E below it.

Option C

Donut with headline

A ring split by state, with the total and the completion rate in the centre. Hovering a state highlights its slice.

Works well for

  • Familiar and appealing; the total is front and centre
  • The legend shows the exact counts

Watch out for

  • Slices of similar size (such as 4 and 5) are hard to compare by eye
  • Takes more space than option B for the same information
Option D

Live case flow

Every case is a dot. When the agent finishes, the business replies or a reviewer decides, its dot moves to the new state. Hover or tab to a dot to see the case and its next action.

The most recently changed case pulses. Moves are animated so you can follow a case.

Works well for

  • The most dynamic option: you can see the agent at work
  • Every case can be reached directly, a good bridge to the case list

Watch out for

  • Gets crowded beyond about 60 cases (would switch to grouped counts)
  • Movement can distract on a screen people work in all day
Option E

Enhanced tiles

Today's five tiles, each with a small bar for its share of the queue and an animated count. The tile highlights briefly when its count changes, and the Agent working icon spins while the agent is busy.

Works well for

  • The smallest change, which keeps the existing layout and click-to-filter
  • Every count stays readable

Watch out for

  • The least dramatic option, so it may not be the "wow" the lead is after
  • Shares across five tiles are harder to compare than in a single bar

Part 2

Views for a leader

Options A–E answer a reviewer's question: what do I work on next? A leader looking at the app asks different questions. This part explains the thinking, then offers four designs (F–I) built for that audience. They use the same live data and the same Simulate activity controls at the top of the page.

Thinking out loud

What a leader needs from this screen

A head of trade operations, a credit risk lead or a judge reviewing the product spends maybe 30 seconds here. They won't open individual cases. They want to know whether the operation is healthy, where the risk is, and whether the agent is worth it.

The five questions a leader asks

  1. How much money is moving, and how much is stuck? Leaders think in exposure (SGD), not case counts. Ten small cases matter less than one large one.
  2. Who is holding things up? Us (the bank), the business, or the agent? Only the first is the leader's problem to fix.
  3. Can I trust the agent? Show what it caught and confirm nothing was cleared with an open issue. This is the hardest question to answer and the most important.
  4. Is it worth it? Cost per case, speed, and how much reviewer time it saves.
  5. What should I do today? One short, ranked list, not a queue.

Design rules that follow from that

  • Lead with the answer. Open with a sentence that says what the numbers mean before any chart. It can be copied straight into an email or a slide.
  • Money first, counts second. Every count gets a value beside it.
  • Show exceptions, not inventory. Highlight the one bar that needs action and grey out the rest.
  • Show evidence, not just a confidence score. For trust, show what was caught, one dot per real case, rather than a single "accuracy" number.
  • Label assumptions. Anything estimated, such as time saved, is an editable input with a visible "assumption" badge.

What I deliberately left out

  • Trend lines and "vs last week" figures. The demo has a single day of history, so any trend would be made up. Add them once there's real history.
  • A single "AI accuracy %" dial. It invites the question "accurate compared to what?" The controls scorecard (H) answers the trust question with real cases.
  • Targets (SLAs). None are defined in the policy yet. Where a threshold appears, as on dial A, it's marked as a placeholder.
Option F

Executive brief

A one-screen summary: a headline sentence, four key numbers and the three cases to act on first, ranked by value. Copy summary puts it on the clipboard as plain text for email or Slack.

Act on these first

Works well for

  • A leader who has 30 seconds
  • Ready to paste into a daily email or a status slide

Watch out for

  • Mostly text and numbers, so less visual; pair it with G or H
  • The headline wording needs agreeing so it reads the same every day
Option G

Where the money is, and who holds it

Invoice value grouped by who holds the next step. Only Waiting on the bank is highlighted, because it's the one a leader can fix. The other groups are context.

Works well for

  • Turns the queue into exposure (SGD), the language a leader thinks in
  • Shows at a glance whether the bank or the business is holding things up

Watch out for

  • Cases whose invoice couldn't be read have no value yet; they're counted, not hidden
  • A real deployment needs currency handling beyond SGD
Option H

Suggestion

A leader page built from these

Rather than choosing one, a "Leader overview" page could combine them from top to bottom:

1. Executive brief (F)The headline sentence and four key numbers, readable in five seconds.
2. Money and controls (G + H)Side by side: where the exposure is and what the agent caught.
3. Business case (I)Further down, or as a separate tab for sponsors.

Reviewers keep the existing work queue (with A, B or E); leaders get this page instead.
